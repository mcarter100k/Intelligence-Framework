# storylines.md — week of 2026-08-03 (approved 2026-08-03)

## GBP — "no cuts" holds until proven otherwise
The pound's story is higher-for-longer: BoE at 3.75%, expected
to hold Thursday for a fifth straight meeting, Bailey saying
cuts are off the table. Inflation just printed a 15-month low
(2.6%) but the Bank itself sees it back above 3% by year-end
with oil pressure building — so the bar for a dovish surprise
is high. The tell on Thursday is the VOTE SPLIT: 7-2 last time;
more dissenters toward cutting is the first crack in this
story. No MPC again until mid-September, so Thursday's guidance
sets six weeks of tone. Bias: supported while "no cuts" holds.
GATE: BoE decision + press conference Thursday — no GBP trades
inside the window, and treat the presser as part of the event.

## AUD — living in the CPI crater until Aug 11
Last week's soft Q2 CPI collapsed August hike odds from ~21%
to ~3-4% and knocked AUD broadly — that repricing is still the
operative story. Underneath it the RBA remains structurally
hawkish (June jobs +76.3K, far above forecast) so a late-year
hike stays alive, but the decision that settles it is NEXT
Tuesday, Aug 11 — nothing scheduled this week. Until then AUD
trades on China, commodities, and risk tone. Bias: soggy;
the GBP/AUD tailwind from the CPI stands unless AU data
surprises hot. Next week inherits the event risk.

## JPY — state-managed market; smallest size or none
The most dangerous board this week. One day removed from
suspected intervention (~600 pips), with authorities publicly
warning they remain ready to act again, and a hawkish BoJ
underneath (8-1 hold, one dissent for 1.25%, consensus for a
hike by December, possibly October). The intervention condition
list is fully lit: multi-decade yen lows, escalating official
rhetoric, one-way positioning freshly punished. Rules: never
chase GBP/JPY longs back toward the pre-crash highs; expect
two-way violence as intervention effects historically fade
against a still-wide rate gap over 1-3 weeks; every JPY setup
answers "who is on the other side" — this week it may be the
state. Bias: choppy and managed. Stand aside is a position.
