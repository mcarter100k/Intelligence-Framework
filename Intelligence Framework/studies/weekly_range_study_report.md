# Weekly range study — 200+ pip weeks vs under 200 (exploratory)

**Span:** weeks of 2021-05-03 → 2026-08-31 (USED partition: learning only, never proof). OANDA mid candles; a week runs Sunday 2 PM PDT (5 PM New York) to Friday 2 PM PDT. Percentages carry 95% confidence intervals; where two groups' intervals overlap heavily, the difference may be noise. Around 15 comparisons per pair are shown, so about one in twenty will look different by chance alone. **Nothing here is an edge**; a pattern found here is a hypothesis to test, pre-registered, on the untouched 2005–2013 data.

## Reading (2026-10-29)

**The 200-pip line splits normal weeks from quiet ones.** 80% of GBP/AUD weeks
and 88% of GBP/JPY weeks were 200+ pips, so a 200+ week is the ordinary
case.

**Real, but not directional — volatility clusters.**
- After a 200+ week, the next week was also 200+ in 83% (GBP/AUD) and 90%
  (GBP/JPY) of cases.
- After a quiet week, 71% and 76%.
- A random walk has no such memory. This is useful for expectations,
  targets and sizing; it says nothing about direction.

**No direction carries over.**
- The next week closed the same way about half the time in every group
  (46–60%, with every confidence interval spanning 50%).
- Knowing how a week closed told you nothing about the next week.

**The weekly shape matches randomness.**
- Trend-shaped weeks, indecision weeks, closes near the high or low,
  extremes set on Monday or Friday, and Monday agreeing with the week all
  land at or near the random-walk numbers.
- Several look like patterns only because 50% is the wrong yardstick:
  - a random week sets its high on Monday or Friday 59% of the time;
  - Monday agrees with the week 65% of the time, because Monday is part of
    the week.
- The two borderline cases are GBP/AUD extremes set midweek (21% vs 16%
  expected) and GBP/JPY Monday agreement (60% vs 64%). Both sit at the edge
  of their confidence intervals, among about 100 comparisons, where several
  borderline results are expected by chance.

**GBP/JPY's lean is the period, not a weekly habit.**
- Up-closing weeks (58% of big weeks, 67% of quieter-than-usual weeks) and
  weekly lows on Monday (40% vs about 30% expected) fit the pair's rise
  from about 150 to 210 over 2021–2026.
- "Calm weeks drift up, stormy weeks crash" has a real reason behind it
  (carry, borrowing yen to buy pounds), but it was a regime. The 2005–2013
  data includes the 2008 carry unwind.

**The "big for its time" comparison with the random walk is not fair, and is
not used.** Real big weeks are often just more volatile. Simulated big weeks
can only be big by moving far in one direction.

**Hypotheses worth a pre-registered test on the untouched 2005–2013 data:**
1. Volatility persistence, as a sizing or target input. It is not an entry
   signal.
2. GBP/JPY quiet-week upward drift (carry). The test must include swap
   and the 2008 unwind.
3. Multi-week trend following. Week-to-week persistence is absent, but
   documented momentum works over 1–12 months, which this study did not
   measure.
